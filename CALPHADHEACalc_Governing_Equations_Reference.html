CALPHADHEACalc: Governing Equations and Variable Reference
Thermodynamic Formulations, Physical Models, and Implementation Notes
Felix Mwiya and Prof. Dr. Sheela Singh
Department of Mechanical Engineering, School of Engineering and Sciences
SRM University-AP, Andhra Pradesh 522240, India
Abstract — This reference manual documents the thermodynamic, mechanical, kinetic, and transport equations implemented in the CALPHADHEACalc software. For each model, we state the governing relationship, the physical meaning and dimensions of its variables, the corresponding software subroutines, and the supporting literature citations. The formulations cover CALPHAD equilibrium thermodynamics, empirical solid-solution criteria, soft-sphere lattice distortion, yield strength superposition, transport properties across the solidus-liquidus window, high-temperature oxidation and electrochemical corrosion kinetics, and kinematic X-ray diffraction.

1. CALPHAD Solution Thermodynamics and Phase Equilibria

Primary Modules: HEAThermoEngine.m, HEATDBEngine.m

1.1 Molar Gibbs Free Energy of Phase φ

Implementation: Evaluated in HEAThermoEngine.m as the thermodynamic objective function minimized during temperature and composition stepping.
$$G_m^\phi(T, \mathbf{c}) = {^{\text{ref}}}G^\phi(T) + {^{\text{id}}}G^\phi(T) + {^{\text{E}}}G^\phi(T) + {^{\text{mag}}}G^\phi(T)$$
Variables:
  • $G_m^\phi(T, \mathbf{c})$: Molar Gibbs free energy of phase $\phi$ at absolute temperature $T$ [$\text{J}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}$].
  • $T$: Absolute temperature [$\text{K}$].
  • $\mathbf{c} = (c_1, c_2, \dots, c_n)$: Mole fraction vector of constituent alloy components [dimensionless, $\sum_{i=1}^n c_i = 1$].
  • ${^{\text{ref}}}G^\phi(T)$: Reference energy of the pure components in the structural state of phase $\phi$ [$\text{J}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}$].
  • ${^{\text{id}}}G^\phi(T)$: Ideal mixing contribution [$\text{J}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}$].
  • ${^{\text{E}}}G^\phi(T)$: Excess Gibbs free energy accounting for atomic pair interactions [$\text{J}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}$].
  • ${^{\text{mag}}}G^\phi(T)$: Magnetic ordering contribution from the Inden-Hillert-Jarl formulation [$\text{J}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}$].

1.2 Reference Energy from SGTE Unary Lattice Stabilities

Implementation: Evaluated in HEATDBEngine.m using polynomial coefficients from the SGTE unary database (Dinsdale, 1991).
$${^{\text{ref}}}G^\phi(T) = \sum_{i=1}^n c_i \, {^\circ}G_i^\phi(T)$$ $${^\circ}G_i^\phi(T) - H_i^{\text{SER}} = a_i + b_i T + c_i T \ln T + d_i T^2 + e_i T^{-1} + f_i T^3$$
Variables:
  • ${^\circ}G_i^\phi(T)$: Standard molar Gibbs energy of pure element $i$ in phase $\phi$ [$\text{J}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}$].
  • $H_i^{\text{SER}}$: Enthalpy of pure element $i$ in its Standard Element Reference (SER) state at $298.15\text{ K}$ and $10^5\text{ Pa}$ [$\text{J}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}$].
  • $a_i, b_i, c_i, d_i, e_i, f_i$: Temperature-dependent polynomial coefficients.
  • $c_i$: Mole fraction of element $i$ [$\text{mol/mol}$].

1.3 Ideal Solution Configurational Entropy

Implementation: Evaluated in HEAThermoEngine.m for random mixing on the crystal sublattice.
$${^{\text{id}}}G^\phi(T) = R T \sum_{i=1}^n c_i \ln c_i$$
Variables:
  • $R$: Universal gas constant, $8.314462 \, \text{J}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}\cdot\text{K}^{-1}$.
  • $T$: Absolute temperature [$\text{K}$].
  • $c_i$: Mole fraction of component $i$ ($0 < c_i \le 1$).

1.4 Redlich-Kister Multicomponent Excess Gibbs Energy

Implementation: Evaluated in HEAThermoEngine.m using binary and ternary Redlich-Kister interaction coefficients with Muggianu geometric extrapolation.
$${^{\text{E}}}G^\phi(T) = \sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\sum_{j=i+1}^n c_i c_j \sum_{k=0}^p L_{ij}^{\phi, (k)}(T) \, (c_i - c_j)^k, \quad L_{ij}^{\phi, (k)}(T) = a_k + b_k T$$
Variables:
  • $L_{ij}^{\phi, (k)}(T)$: $k$-th order binary interaction parameter between elements $i$ and $j$ in phase $\phi$ [$\text{J}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}$].
  • $a_k$: Enthalpic interaction coefficient [$\text{J}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}$].
  • $b_k$: Entropic interaction coefficient [$\text{J}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}\cdot\text{K}^{-1}$].
  • $p$: Expansion order ($p = 0$ for regular solutions; $p \ge 1$ for sub-regular solutions).

1.5 Chemical Potential Balance and Multiphase Equilibrium

Implementation: Solved in HEAThermoEngine.m using a constrained Newton-Raphson scheme to match chemical potentials under mass conservation.
$$\mu_i^\alpha(T, \mathbf{x}^\alpha) = \mu_i^\beta(T, \mathbf{x}^\beta) = \dots = \mu_i^\nu(T, \mathbf{x}^\nu) \quad (\forall i = 1, \dots, n)$$ $$\sum_{\phi=1}^\Phi f_\phi x_i^\phi = c_i^0, \quad \sum_{\phi=1}^\Phi f_\phi = 1$$
Variables:
  • $\mu_i^\phi$: Chemical potential of component $i$ in phase $\phi$ [$\text{J}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}$], where $\mu_i^\phi = (\partial G^\phi / \partial n_i)_{T, P, n_{j \ne i}}$.
  • $f_\phi$: Molar phase fraction of phase $\phi$ ($0 \le f_\phi \le 1$).
  • $\mathbf{x}^\phi = (x_1^\phi, \dots, x_n^\phi)$: Composition of phase $\phi$ [$\text{mol/mol}$].
  • $c_i^0$: Overall alloy mole fraction of component $i$.
  • $\alpha, \beta, \dots, \nu$: Equilibrium phases (e.g. FCC\_A1, BCC\_A2, B2, Liquid, Sigma).

2. Empirical Solid-Solution Criteria and Solidification

Primary Module: HEAThermoEngine.m

2.1 Miedema Enthalpy of Mixing and Configurational Entropy

Implementation: Evaluated in HEAThermoEngine.m using Miedema regular-solution interaction enthalpies.
$$\Delta H_{\text{mix}} = \sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\sum_{j=i+1}^n 4 \, \Omega_{ij} \, c_i \, c_j, \quad \Delta S_{\text{mix}} = -R \sum_{i=1}^n c_i \ln c_i$$
Variables:
  • $\Delta H_{\text{mix}}$: Molar enthalpy of mixing [$\text{kJ}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}$].
  • $\Omega_{ij}$: Equiatomic binary mixing enthalpy between elements $i$ and $j$ [$\text{kJ}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}$].
  • $\Delta S_{\text{mix}}$: Configurational entropy of mixing [$\text{J}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}\cdot\text{K}^{-1}$].
  • $c_i, c_j$: Atomic fractions of components $i$ and $j$.

2.2 Atomic Size Mismatch (δ) and Electronegativity Difference (Δχ)

Implementation: Evaluated in HEAThermoEngine.m to assess Hume-Rothery topological strain criteria ($\delta \le 6.6\%$) and chemical electronegativity differences.
$$\delta = \sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^n c_i \left(1 - \frac{r_i}{\bar{r}}\right)^2} \times 100\%, \quad \bar{r} = \sum_{i=1}^n c_i r_i$$ $$\Delta\chi = \sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^n c_i \left(\chi_i - \bar{\chi}\right)^2}, \quad \bar{\chi} = \sum_{i=1}^n c_i \chi_i$$
Variables:
  • $\delta$: Atomic size difference parameter [$\%$].
  • $r_i$: Goldschmidt atomic radius of element $i$ [$\text{\AA}$].
  • $\bar{r}$: Average atomic radius of the alloy system [$\text{\AA}$].
  • $\chi_i$: Pauling electronegativity of element $i$.
  • $\Delta\chi$: Root-mean-square electronegativity mismatch.

2.3 Yang-Zhang Thermodynamic Parameter (Ω)

Implementation: Evaluated in HEAThermoEngine.m using the Yang-Zhang criterion ($\Omega \ge 1.1$ and $\delta \le 6.6\%$).
$$\Omega = \frac{T_m \, \Delta S_{\text{mix}}}{|\Delta H_{\text{mix}}|}, \quad T_m = \sum_{i=1}^n c_i T_{m,i}$$
Variables:
  • $\Omega$: Thermodynamic stability parameter [dimensionless].
  • $T_m$: Rule-of-mixtures melting temperature [$\text{K}$].
  • $T_{m,i}$: Melting point of pure component $i$ [$\text{K}$].

2.4 Guo Valence Electron Concentration (VEC) Criteria

Implementation: Evaluated in HEAThermoEngine.m following the phase boundary criteria of Guo et al.: $\text{VEC} \ge 8.0$ favors FCC; $\text{VEC} < 6.87$ favors BCC; $6.87 \le \text{VEC} < 8.0$ indicates mixed FCC+BCC.
$$\text{VEC} = \sum_{i=1}^n c_i \, \text{VEC}_i$$
Variables:
  • $\text{VEC}$: Valence electron concentration of the alloy [electrons/atom].
  • $\text{VEC}_i$: Valence electron count of element $i$.

2.5 Scheil-Gulliver Solidification and Homogenization Kinetics

Implementation: Evaluated in HEAThermoEngine.m to estimate solute segregation under rapid cooling and calculate homogenization heat treatment conditions.
$$T_{\text{ScheilSolidus}} = T_{\text{Solidus}} - 0.45 \cdot (T_{\text{Liquidus}} - T_{\text{Solidus}}) \cdot (1 + 0.05 \cdot \delta)$$ $$I_{\text{seg}} = 0.25 + 0.06 \cdot \delta, \quad T_{\text{homog}} = \min(T_{\text{Solidus}} - 50 \text{ K}, \, 0.88 \cdot T_{\text{Solidus}}), \quad \tau_{\text{homog}} = 10.0 \cdot \left(\frac{I_{\text{seg}}}{0.35}\right)^2 \text{ hours}$$
Variables:
  • $T_{\text{ScheilSolidus}}$: Non-equilibrium solidus temperature [$\text{K}$].
  • $T_{\text{Solidus}}, T_{\text{Liquidus}}$: Equilibrium solidus and liquidus temperatures [$\text{K}$].
  • $I_{\text{seg}}$: Segregation severity index.
  • $T_{\text{homog}}$: Annealing homogenization temperature [$\text{K}$].
  • $\tau_{\text{homog}}$: Isothermal soaking time [$\text{hours}$].

3. Lattice Distortion and Dislocation Mechanics

Primary Module: HEALatticeDistortionEngine.m

3.1 Effective Relaxed Atomic Radius

Implementation: Evaluated in HEALatticeDistortionEngine.m following the elastic soft-sphere model of He and Yang (2018).
$$r_i^* = r_i \left(1 - \beta \frac{K_i - \bar{K}}{\bar{K}}\right), \quad \beta = \frac{1 + \nu}{3(1 - \nu)}, \quad \bar{K} = \sum_{i=1}^n c_i K_i$$
Variables:
  • $r_i^*$: Effective relaxed atomic radius of component $i$ in the solid solution [$\text{\AA}$].
  • $r_i$: Unperturbed metallic radius [$\text{\AA}$].
  • $K_i, \bar{K}$: Bulk modulus of component $i$ and composition-weighted average bulk modulus [$\text{GPa}$].
  • $\nu$: Average Poisson's ratio ($\approx 0.30 - 0.33$).
  • $\beta$: Elastic stress accommodation factor.

3.2 Root-Mean-Square Lattice Strain

Implementation: Evaluated in HEALatticeDistortionEngine.m to quantify static atomic displacements.
$$\varepsilon_{\text{RMS}} = \sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^n c_i \left(\frac{r_i^* - \bar{r}^*}{\bar{r}^*}\right)^2}, \quad \bar{r}^* = \sum_{i=1}^n c_i r_i^*$$
Variables:
  • $\varepsilon_{\text{RMS}}$: Root-mean-square static displacement strain [dimensionless].
  • $\bar{r}^*$: Mean effective atomic radius [$\text{\AA}$].

3.3 Theoretical Shear Strain and Collective Solute Pinning

Implementation: Evaluated in HEALatticeDistortionEngine.m to compute theoretical shear resistance and the Labusch solute pinning increment.
$$\bar{\gamma}_{\text{th}} = \gamma_0 \left(1 - \alpha_{\text{dist}} \varepsilon_{\text{RMS}}\right)$$ $$\tau_c = \tau_{\text{Peierls}} + \Delta\tau_{\text{Labusch}}, \quad \Delta\tau_{\text{Labusch}} = A_L \cdot G \cdot \varepsilon_{\text{RMS}}^{4/3} \cdot c_i^{2/3}$$
Variables:
  • $\bar{\gamma}_{\text{th}}$: Theoretical plastic shear strain limit.
  • $\gamma_0$: Ideal shear strain of unperturbed lattice ($\approx 0.11$).
  • $\alpha_{\text{dist}}$: Lattice distortion coupling coefficient ($\approx 2.8$).
  • $\tau_c$: Critical resolved shear stress [$\text{MPa}$].
  • $\tau_{\text{Peierls}}$: Intrinsic lattice friction stress [$\text{MPa}$].
  • $\Delta\tau_{\text{Labusch}}$: Solid-solution strengthening increment [$\text{MPa}$].
  • $G$: Shear modulus [$\text{GPa}$].
  • $A_L$: Dimensionless scaling factor.

3.4 Dislocation Activation Volume

Implementation: Evaluated in HEALatticeDistortionEngine.m for thermally activated dislocation glide.
$$V^* = b \cdot d_w \cdot l_s = -\frac{\partial \Delta G^*}{\partial \tau}$$
Variables:
  • $V^*$: Dislocation activation volume [$\text{m}^3$ or in units of $b^3$].
  • $b$: Magnitude of Burgers vector [$\text{nm}$] ($\approx 0.255\text{ nm}$ for FCC, $\approx 0.286\text{ nm}$ for BCC).
  • $d_w$: Obstacle barrier width [$\text{nm}$].
  • $l_s$: Average spacing between pinning obstacles [$\text{nm}$].

4. Mechanical Strengthening and Constitutive Relations

Primary Module: HEAMechanicalEngine.m

4.1 Yield Strength Superposition

Implementation: Evaluated in HEAMechanicalEngine.m to calculate 0.2% offset engineering tensile yield strength.
$$\sigma_y = \sigma_0 + \Delta\sigma_{\text{ss}} + \Delta\sigma_{\text{HP}} + \Delta\sigma_{\text{ppt}}$$
Variables:
  • $\sigma_y$: Engineering yield strength [$\text{MPa}$].
  • $\sigma_0$: Lattice friction stress [$\text{MPa}$] ($\sim 45\text{ MPa}$ for FCC; $\sim 650 - 950\text{ MPa}$ for BCC).
  • $\Delta\sigma_{\text{ss}}$: Solid-solution strengthening contribution [$\text{MPa}$].
  • $\Delta\sigma_{\text{HP}}$: Hall-Petch grain boundary strengthening [$\text{MPa}$].
  • $\Delta\sigma_{\text{ppt}}$: Precipitation strengthening contribution [$\text{MPa}$].

4.2 Solid Solution Strengthening: Varvenne-Curtin (FCC) and Maresca-Curtin (BCC)

Implementation: Evaluated in HEAMechanicalEngine.m based on the random alloy strengthening theories of Varvenne et al. (2016) and Maresca and Curtin (2020).
$$\Delta\sigma_{\text{ss}}^{\text{FCC}} = 0.0405 \cdot M \cdot \mu \left(\frac{1 + \nu}{1 - \nu}\right)^{4/3} \left[ \sum_{i=1}^n c_i \left(\frac{\Delta V_i}{V}\right)^2 \right]^{2/3}$$ $$\Delta\sigma_{\text{ss}}^{\text{BCC}} = M \cdot \left[ \tau_{\text{kink}} + \alpha_k \cdot \mu \cdot \Delta\varepsilon_{\text{size}}^{4/3} \right] $$
Variables:
  • $M$: Taylor orientation factor ($M = 3.06$ for FCC; $M = 2.73$ for BCC).
  • $\mu$: Shear modulus [$\text{GPa}$].
  • $\nu$: Poisson's ratio.
  • $\Delta V_i / V$: Normalized misfit volume of solute element $i$.
  • $\tau_{\text{kink}}$: Stress threshold for screw dislocation kink-pair nucleation [$\text{MPa}$].

4.3 Hall-Petch Hardening and High-Temperature Softening

Implementation: Evaluated in HEAMechanicalEngine.m for grain-size hardening and thermal softening up to the melting point.
$$\Delta\sigma_{\text{HP}} = \frac{k_{\text{HP}}}{\sqrt{d}}, \quad \sigma_y(T) = \sigma_y(298\text{ K}) \left[ 1 - \left(\frac{T - 298}{T_m - 298}\right)^m \right]$$
Variables:
  • $k_{\text{HP}}$: Hall-Petch coefficient [$\text{MPa}\cdot\mu\text{m}^{1/2}$] ($494 \, \text{MPa}\cdot\mu\text{m}^{1/2}$ for CoCrFeMnNi).
  • $d$: Mean grain diameter [$\mu\text{m}$] ($30 \, \mu\text{m}$ for standard reference state).
  • $T_m$: Solidus temperature [$\text{K}$].
  • $m$: Thermal softening exponent ($0.65 - 0.85$).

4.4 Hardness, True Flow Stress, and Wear Loss

Implementation: Evaluated in HEAMechanicalEngine.m for Vickers microhardness, Hollomon work-hardening, Archard wear loss, and cladding dilution.
$$\text{HV} \approx 0.3 \, \sigma_y + 60, \quad \sigma_{\text{true}} = K \, \varepsilon_{\text{true}}^n, \quad W_{\text{wear}} = K_{\text{Archard}} \frac{F_N \, s}{H}, \quad \eta = \frac{A_{\text{sub}}}{A_{\text{clad}} + A_{\text{sub}}} \times 100\%$$
Variables:
  • $\text{HV}$: Vickers microhardness [$\text{kgf}\cdot\text{mm}^{-2}$].
  • $K$: Hollomon strength coefficient [$\text{MPa}$].
  • $n$: Strain-hardening exponent.
  • $W_{\text{wear}}$: Volumetric wear loss [$\text{mm}^3$].
  • $K_{\text{Archard}}$: Dimensionless Archard wear coefficient.
  • $F_N$: Normal load [$\text{N}$].
  • $s$: Sliding distance [$\text{m}$].
  • $\eta$: Substrate dilution fraction [$\%$].

5. Thermophysical Transport Properties

Primary Module: HEATransportEngine.m

5.1 Density, Specific Heat, and Thermal Conductivity

Implementation: Evaluated in HEATransportEngine.m for temperature-dependent density, Kopp-Neumann heat capacity, and thermal conductivity.
$$\rho(T) = \frac{\rho(298\text{ K})}{1 + 3 \, \alpha_L (T - 298)}, \quad C_p(T) = \sum_{i=1}^n c_i \, C_{p, i}(T), \quad k(T) = \frac{L_0 \, T}{\rho_{\text{el}}(T)} + \frac{A_{\text{ph}}}{T}$$
Variables:
  • $\rho(T)$: Density at temperature $T$ [$\text{kg}\cdot\text{m}^{-3}$].
  • $\alpha_L$: Linear coefficient of thermal expansion [$\text{K}^{-1}$].
  • $C_p(T)$: Molar heat capacity at constant pressure [$\text{J}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}\cdot\text{K}^{-1}$].
  • $k(T)$: Total thermal conductivity [$\text{W}\cdot\text{m}^{-1}\cdot\text{K}^{-1}$].
  • $L_0$: Sommerfeld Lorenz number, $2.443 \times 10^{-8} \, \text{W}\cdot\Omega\cdot\text{K}^{-2}$.
  • $\rho_{\text{el}}(T)$: Electrical resistivity [$\Omega\cdot\text{m}$].

5.2 Semi-Solid Viscosity and Laser Absorptivity

Implementation: Evaluated in HEATransportEngine.m using the Krieger-Dougherty semi-solid rheology relation and Hagen-Rubens optical absorptivity.
$$\eta(f_s) = \eta_{\text{liq}} \left(1 - \frac{f_s}{f_{s, \text{crit}}}\right)^{-2.5 \, f_{s, \text{crit}}}, \quad A_{\text{laser}}(\lambda, T) \approx \sqrt{\frac{8 \pi \varepsilon_0 c_0 \, \rho_{\text{el}}(T)}{\lambda}}$$
Variables:
  • $\eta(f_s)$: Dynamic apparent viscosity in the mushy zone [$\text{Pa}\cdot\text{s}$].
  • $\eta_{\text{liq}}$: Molten liquid viscosity ($\approx 0.003 - 0.006 \, \text{Pa}\cdot\text{s}$).
  • $f_{s, \text{crit}}$: Critical solid fraction at rheological jamming ($0.50 - 0.60$).
  • $A_{\text{laser}}$: Optical surface absorptivity [dimensionless].
  • $\lambda$: Laser wavelength [$\text{m}$] ($1064\text{ nm}$ for Nd:YAG).

6. Environmental Kinetics: Oxidation and Corrosion

Primary Modules: HEAOxidationEngine.m, HEACorrosionEngine.m

6.1 Wagner Parabolic Oxidation and Pilling-Bedworth Ratio

Implementation: Evaluated in HEAOxidationEngine.m for isothermal oxidation mass gain curves and oxide scale compactness.
$$\left(\frac{\Delta W}{A}\right)^2 = k_p(T) \, t, \quad k_p(T) = k_0 \exp\left(-\frac{Q_{\text{ox}}}{R T}\right), \quad \text{PBR} = \frac{M_{\text{ox}} \, \rho_{\text{met}}}{n_{\text{met}} \, M_{\text{met}} \, \rho_{\text{ox}}}$$
Variables:
  • $\Delta W / A$: Mass gain per unit area [$\text{mg}\cdot\text{cm}^{-2}$].
  • $k_p(T)$: Parabolic rate constant [$\text{mg}^2\cdot\text{cm}^{-4}\cdot\text{h}^{-1}$].
  • $t$: Exposure duration [$\text{hours}$].
  • $Q_{\text{ox}}$: Oxidation activation energy [$\text{kJ}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}$].
  • $\text{PBR}$: Pilling-Bedworth ratio ($1.0 \le \text{PBR} \le 2.0$ indicates protective scale).

6.2 Electrochemical Corrosion Rate (ASTM G102) and PREN

Implementation: Evaluated in HEACorrosionEngine.m according to ASTM G102-89 and Butler-Volmer polarization kinetics.
$$\text{CR} = K_{\text{corr}} \, \frac{i_{\text{corr}} \, \text{EW}}{\rho}, \quad \text{EW} = \left[ \sum_{i=1}^n \frac{w_i \, n_i}{M_i} \right]^{-1}, \quad \text{PREN}_{\text{HEA}} = \text{wt}\%\text{Cr} + 3.3 (\text{wt}\%\text{Mo} + 0.5\,\text{wt}\%\text{W}) + 16\,\text{wt}\%\text{N}$$ $$i_{\text{net}} = i_{\text{corr}} \left[ \exp\left(\frac{2.303(E - E_{\text{corr}})}{\beta_a}\right) - \exp\left(-\frac{2.303(E - E_{\text{corr}})}{\beta_c}\right) \right]$$
Variables:
  • $\text{CR}$: Annual corrosion penetration rate [$\text{mm}\cdot\text{year}^{-1}$].
  • $K_{\text{corr}}$: Conversion factor, $0.00327 \, \text{mm}\cdot\text{g}\cdot\mu\text{A}^{-1}\cdot\text{cm}^{-1}\cdot\text{year}^{-1}$.
  • $i_{\text{corr}}$: Corrosion current density [$\mu\text{A}\cdot\text{cm}^{-2}$].
  • $\text{EW}$: Alloy equivalent weight [$\text{g}\cdot\text{equivalent}^{-1}$].
  • $w_i$: Mass fraction of element $i$.
  • $n_i$: Primary valence state in dissolution (e.g. Fe: 2, Cr: 3, Ni: 2, Mo: 4).
  • $\text{PREN}_{\text{HEA}}$: Pitting resistance equivalent number.
  • $\beta_a, \beta_c$: Anodic and cathodic Tafel slopes [$\text{V}\cdot\text{decade}^{-1}$].

7. Kinematic Powder X-Ray Diffractometry

Primary Module: HEAXRDEngine.m

7.1 Bragg Diffraction and Structure Factor

Implementation: Evaluated in HEAXRDEngine.m to synthesize kinematic X-ray diffraction patterns for cubic and hexagonal phases.
$$\lambda = 2 \, d_{hkl} \, \sin\theta_{hkl}, \quad d_{hkl} = \frac{a_0}{\sqrt{h^2 + k^2 + l^2}}$$ $$I_{hkl} \propto |F_{hkl}|^2 \cdot p_{hkl} \cdot \left(\frac{1 + \cos^2(2\theta)}{\sin^2\theta \cos\theta}\right) \cdot \exp(-2 B_{\text{DW}} \sin^2\theta / \lambda^2)$$
Variables:
  • $\lambda$: X-ray wavelength [$\text{\AA}$] ($1.54056 \, \text{\AA}$ for $\text{Cu-K}\alpha_1$).
  • $\theta_{hkl}$: Bragg angle [$\text{degrees}$].
  • $d_{hkl}$: Interplanar spacing of $(hkl)$ planes [$\text{\AA}$].
  • $a_0$: Lattice parameter from Vegard's law with soft-sphere relaxation [$\text{\AA}$].
  • $F_{hkl}$: Structure factor for reflection $(hkl)$.
  • $p_{hkl}$: Multiplicity factor of the reflection plane family.
  • $B_{\text{DW}}$: Debye-Waller parameter [$\text{\AA}^2$].

7.2 Peak Broadening: Scherrer and Williamson-Hall

Implementation: Evaluated in HEAXRDEngine.m to simulate line broadening from finite crystallite size and lattice microstrain.
$$\text{FWHM}_{hkl} = \frac{K_{\text{Scherrer}} \, \lambda}{D_{\text{crystallite}} \cos\theta_{hkl}} + 4 \, \varepsilon_{\text{micro}} \tan\theta_{hkl}$$
Variables:
  • $\text{FWHM}_{hkl}$: Peak full width at half maximum [$\text{rad}$].
  • $K_{\text{Scherrer}}$: Scherrer shape factor ($\approx 0.94$).
  • $D_{\text{crystallite}}$: Coherent domain size [$\text{nm}$].
  • $\varepsilon_{\text{micro}}$: Root-mean-square microstrain.

8. Application Ranking and COMSOL Export

Primary Modules: HEAApplicationDatabase.m, CALPHADHEACalc.m

8.1 Multi-Criteria Suitability Scoring

Implementation: Evaluated in HEAApplicationDatabase.m to rank candidate alloys across 8 engineering application sectors.
$$S_j = \left[ \sum_{k=1}^m w_k \cdot \phi_k(P_k) \right] \times \prod_{l=1}^p \Theta_l, \quad \phi_k(P_k) = \frac{1}{1 + \exp\left(-\kappa_k \cdot \frac{P_k - P_{k, \text{target}}}{\Delta P_k}\right)}$$
Variables:
  • $S_j$: Overall suitability score for sector $j$ ($0 \le S_j \le 1.0$).
  • $w_k$: Weighting factor for property $k$ ($\sum w_k = 1.0$).
  • $\phi_k(P_k)$: Sigmoidal response function for property $P_k \in [0, 1]$.
  • $\Theta_l \in \{0, 1\}$: Hard constraint flag (e.g. elongation $< 5\%$ disqualifies cryogenic applications).

8.2 Continuous Piecewise Property Smoothing for COMSOL

Implementation: Evaluated in CALPHADHEACalc.m to export continuous material property functions for finite element simulations.
$$P_{\text{smooth}}(T) = P_{\text{solid}}(T) + \left[P_{\text{liquid}}(T) - P_{\text{solid}}(T)\right] \cdot \text{flc2hs}\left(T - T_{\text{Solidus}}, \, \Delta T_{\text{smooth}}\right)$$
Variables:
  • $P_{\text{smooth}}(T)$: Continuous property function across solid, mushy, and liquid regimes.
  • $\text{flc2hs}(x, \Delta x)$: COMSOL smoothed Heaviside function with continuous second derivative.
  • $\Delta T_{\text{smooth}}$: Smoothing transition temperature width [$\text{K}$].
